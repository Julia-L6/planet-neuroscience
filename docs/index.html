    Sparse Firing and Connectivity in Neurogenesis Improve Pattern Separation and Memory in a Simulated Dentate Gyrus

    ABSTRACT

    Adult neurogenesis, the birth of new neurons continuing into adulthood, is believed to play a role in pattern separation during the encoding of episodic memories, particularly in the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus. However, computational models of the dentate gyrus that incorporate neurogenesis have shown inconsistent results and often omit learning and memory processes. We incorporated the dynamics of aging dentate granule cells into a spiking restricted Boltzmann machine model of the dentate gyrus to study the effects of neurogenesis on pattern separation and memory interference. Our simulations show that including neurogenesis and modeling the detailed changes over the granule cell life cycle leads to a general improvement in pattern separation. At the same time, both proactive and retroactive interference are greatly reduced. These results support the view that sparse firing in mature granule cells and sparse connectivity in young granule cells are critical factors in establishing a functional role for neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus.

    Identification of Kinome Biomarkers and Therapeutic Rescue in cGMP-Linked Retinal Degeneration

    Inherited retinal degenerations, such as Retinitis Pigmentosa (RP) and Leber's congenital amaurosis (LCA), are major causes of progressive and irreversible vision loss. The large number of genes associated with these disorders makes their clinical and molecular characterization difficult, while systemic biomarkers for disease progression or treatment response remain limited. These challenges were addressed by targeting cGMP-associated mechanisms shared by RP and LCA and integrating systemic biomarker profiling with retinal outcomes. Kinase activity analysis (kinome analysis) in peripheral blood mononuclear cells distinguished healthy and diseased states and captured therapy-associated signatures in rd10 mice treated with LP-CN03, a liposomal formulation of the cGMP analog CN03. This first systemic analysis in a therapeutically responsive animal model highlights kinome analysis as a potential translational readout. Given shared cGMP signaling in RP and AIPL1-associated LCA, LP-CN03's therapeutic potential was further evaluated in a new CRISPR-Cas9-generated Aipl1-/- mouse model. Treatment with LP-CN03 preserved retinal structure and function, as shown by electroretinography, longitudinal optical coherence tomography, fundus imaging, optomotor testing, and photoreceptor-specific gene expression. Together, these findings provide independent in vivo evidence for the neuroprotective effect of LP-CN03 in both cGMP-related RP and LCA, also highlighting the translational value of kinome profiling.

    Feasible closed-loop stimulation for neural latent dynamics control

    Modern neuroscience benefits from powerful computational and experimental methods for studying neural populations, including latent dynamics models that predict behavior with few dimensions. But despite their explanatory utility, latent factors are seldom targeted explicitly with experimental perturbations, leaving causal relationships to behavior and inferred dynamical landscapes untested. A promising solution is closed-loop, multi-channel optogenetic control, which, when paired with model predictions, could steer neural trajectories in real time--a feat impossible for traditional, hand-designed stimulation. However, because closed-loop latent control has not been demonstrated in vivo, the hardware and software requirements remain unclear--particularly the necessary stimulation dimensionality and how limited resources such as implant volume and bandwidth should be allocated between stimulation and recording capabilities. Preliminary experiments would thus help guide ongoing technological development. As large-scale exploration of these design spaces in vivo is prohibitively expensive, we conducted a high-throughput simulation survey using biophysically grounded spiking neural networks. We found that low-dimensional actuation is sufficient: in the reference configuration, ~20% of the stimulation channels achieved ~85% of peak control performance, far fewer than the number of neurons. Furthermore, allocating channels roughly equally between stimulation and recording optimized behavioral control, whereas pairing many electrodes with a single light source--the prevailing practice in experimental labs--performed poorly. Even without heavily optimizing modeling or control, we obtained >60% tracking error reduction and >90% evoked behavior accuracy on average for optimal channel counts. These results held across extensive parameter variations, suggesting that closed-loop latent control is feasible with accessible channel counts and establishing design principles for future tool development and in vivo experiments.

    Shikonin alleviates bone cancer pain through suppression of spinal IGF2-associated p38 MAPK signaling

    Background Bone cancer pain (BCP) is a debilitating complication of advanced cancer that substantially impairs physical function and emotional wellbeing. Current pharmacological treatments often provide incomplete analgesia and may be limited by adverse effects. Shikonin, a bioactive naphthoquinone derived from Lithospermum erythrorhizon, exhibits analgesic and anti inflammatory activities, but its effects and underlying mechanisms in BCP remain unclear. Methods A rat model of BCP was established by intratibial inoculation of luciferase labeled MADB106 cells. The analgesic and behavioral effects of intrathecal Shikonin were evaluated using mechanical and thermal nociceptive assays, CatWalk gait analysis, the open field test (OFT), elevated plus maze (EPM), light/dark box (LDB) test, and rotarod testing. Bioluminescence imaging and hematoxylin eosin staining were used to characterize tumor development and bone pathology. Spinal dorsal horn transcriptomic sequencing was performed to identify candidate molecular pathways. Western blotting, quantitative PCR, and ELISA were used to examine IGF2 associated p38 MAPK signaling and proinflammatory cytokines. The functional involvement of IGF2 and p38 associated signaling was further evaluated using intrathecal IGF2 siRNA and the p38 inhibitor Adezmapimod (SB203580). Results Intrathecal Shikonin produced dose and time dependent antinociceptive effects in BCP rats, with an estimated ED50 of 29.80 g/kg, and did not produce an apparent impairment of rotarod performance. Shikonin also improved gait abnormalities and pain related anxiety like behaviors. Transcriptomic analysis identified Igf2 as a candidate gene responsive to Shikonin treatment, and spinal IGF2 and p38 phosphorylation were increased during BCP development. Shikonin significantly reduced IGF2 expression and p38 phosphorylation in the spinal dorsal horn, accompanied by decreased IL 6, IL 1{beta};, and TNF ; expression in spinal tissue. IGF2 knockdown reproduced the analgesic and behavioral effects of Shikonin and reduced p38 phosphorylation, whereas p38 inhibition reproduced key behavioral effects without a marked change in IGF2 expression. Combined treatment with Shikonin and either IGF2 knockdown or p38 inhibition produced no significant additional behavioral benefit. Conclusion Intrathecal Shikonin alleviates nociceptive hypersensitivity, functional impairment, and pain related anxiety like behaviors in rats with MADB106 induced BCP. The phenocopying and non additive effects of IGF2 knockdown and p38 inhibition support the involvement of an IGF2 associated p38 MAPK signaling axis in the analgesic actions of Shikonin.

    Dual kinase inhibition provides early clues to restoration of neuroimmune homeostasis in an AAV-haSyn model of Parkinsons disease

    Background Previous studies of drugs targeting mixed linage kinase type 3 (MLK3) and/or leucine rich repeat kinase type 2 (LRRK2) in sporadic Parkinsons disease (PD) have failed to meet clinical endpoints despite multiple lines of evidence implicating these kinases in pathogenesis. This has reduced enthusiasm for MLK3 and LRRK2 as druggable targets. Largely overlooked is the evidence that these targets remain valid but must be considered in combination to achieve a therapeutic outcome. This provides the rationale for this study. Methods We conducted a pilot study modeling early, pre-symptomatic PD with subsequent evaluation of therapeutic potential of a multi-kinase inhibitor (URMC-099) in alleviating disease pathology. Sprague Dawley rats received unilateral intranigral injections of low dose AAV-haSyn. After a two-week recovery period, all animals were administered either once-daily intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of URMC-099 or its vehicle control until their terminal endpoint. Induction of early PD, and its alleviation with URMC-099, was evaluated by lateralized motor impairment using the cylinder test and immunohistochemical analysis of neuropathology in the substantia nigra (SN) and striatum. Finally, an in vitro microglial PD model exposed to aSyn pre-formed fibrils and treated with URMC-099 was used to further evaluate the mechanism underlying therapeutic activity. Results A unilateral model of early and progressive AAV-haSyn model for PD was used to demonstrate the therapeutic potential of a targeted multi-kinase inhibitor to attenuate PD-associated neuropathology and prevent the manifestation of lateral motor deficits. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed protection of neuronal cellular integrity along the nigral-striatal pathway with once-daily i.p. URMC-099. The mitigation of degenerative neuropathology was linked to targeted inhibition of MLK3 and LRRK2 and complemented by reduced levels of gliosis in astrocytes and microglia in the SN. Single-microglia segmentation analysis suggested URMC-099 treatment preserved dynamic effector state switching in microglia to enhance autophagic flux to reduce the overall burden of pathologic aSyn. Transcriptomic analysis of a complementary in vitro microglia model corroborated the retention of a homeostatic-like effector state with URMC-099 treatment that was unique to MLK3/LRRK2 dual inhibition. Conclusions Unilateral injection of low dose AAV-haSyn successfully resulted in a lateralized model of early, progressive PD. Dual inhibition of aberrant MLK3 and LRRK2 activity with once-daily i.p. URMC-099 therapy significantly mitigated the development of PD pathology and progression in an early manifest phenotype. Our results present a potential mechanism of therapeutic action dependent on the dual inhibition of MLK3 and LRRK2. This presents a novel therapeutic modality that may be beneficial to a broad population of etiologic PD patients.

    PINN-based functional inference in size-structured population models: Application to fish oogenesis

    by Louis Fostier, Manon Lesage, Violette Thermes, Frédérique Clément, Romain Yvinec

    Early oogenesis in juvenile fish establishes the ovarian reserve, which conditions lifelong reproductive capacity. This process is regulated by local hormonal feedback mechanisms, mainly involving Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH). In this work, we develop a mechanistic size-structured population model describing an unstructured germ-cell compartment and a population of growing oocytes, with AMH feedback on the renewal of the precursor cells compartment. Using repeated cross-sectional observations of oocyte size distributions in fish ovaries, we formulate and solve a nonlinear inverse problem with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to infer three demographic functions: the size-dependent oocyte growth rate, the AMH-regulated net renewal rate of the precursor cells compartment, and the recruitment rate of new oocytes. We introduce an auxiliary PINN formulation to handle the nonlocal interaction term and jointly infer unobserved state variables and demographic functions. Our inference strategy combines non-parametric PINNs with a data-guided model-selection step: demographic functions are first inferred without prescribing their functional forms, and the learned profiles are then used to select parsimonious parametric laws, whose parameters are subsequently estimated within a parametric PINN. We investigate identifiability at both the functional and parametric levels, combining structural identifiability analysis with synthetic-data experiments assessing the practical recovery of functions and parameters. The inference procedure is embedded within an individual-level bootstrap framework to assess sensitivity to repeated-cross-sectional sampling and stochastic training. Applied to juvenile fish oogenesis, the framework recovers interpretable growth and renewal dynamics, provides access to unobserved quantities, and enables conditional in silico investigation. More broadly, this work provides a framework for functional and parametric inference in size-structured population models with nonlocal interactions from sparse cross-sectional data.

    by Rana A. Barghout, Lya Chiñas Serrano, Benjamin Sanchez-Lengeling, Radhakrishnan Mahadevan

    The construction of accurate enzyme-constrained genome-scale models (ecGEMs) remains a critical challenge in systems biology, limited by sparse kinetic data and the need for biologically meaningful representations. These innovations breakdown the barrier to application of ecGEMs to non-model organisms and open new avenues for metabolic engineering and synthetic biology in more industrially relevant hosts.

    Sequence-based coevolutionary prediction of species-specific interactomes

    by Miguel Fernandez-Martin, Camila Pontes, Victoria Ruiz-Serra, Alfonso Valencia

    Evolutionarily conserved protein-protein interactions (PPIs) reveal fundamental biological processes. However, predicting protein function solely from these interactions provides an incomplete picture of biological systems. Computational methods for predicting PPIs often struggle due to limited functional annotations in databases, making it difficult to fully understand unique biological systems. This study introduces ContextMirror2.0 (CM2.0), a coevolution-based method designed to address these limitations. Coevolutionary approaches, unlike supervised machine learning methods, do not rely on labelled datasets, proving valuable in addressing the scarcity of data for species-specific PPIs. Around 40% of CM2.0’s top-1000 predicted PPIs for E. coli, S. enterica, and S. aureus are supported by experimental PPI databases. However, comparative analysis of the resulting functional communities further revealed both highly conserved interaction patterns and species-specific interactions. CM2.0 leverages coevolutionary information at the phylogenetic level to explore protein interaction dynamics and understand the functional consequences of species-specific variations. This information can inform further studies on the emergence of novel functions, adaptation to specific environments, and the development of targeted therapies.

    SwimmingIndividuals: A high-performance agent-based model for marine ecosystems

    by Matthew S. Woodstock, Jann Paul Mattern, Zhen Wu, Gregory L. Britten

    Because they are based on established theory, mechanistic models are essential for projecting ecological responses to novel conditions. Yet mechanistic agent-based model (ABM) applications have often been limited by computational constraints. This paper introduces SwimmingIndividuals, an open-source ABM framework that advances our ability to simulate physiological processes, animal movement, higher trophic level marine ecosystem dynamics, and fisheries removals at large ecological scales. The software's key contributions are a suite of mechanistic sub-models governing the full life cycle of each agent, including visual prey detection, adaptive behaviors such as diel vertical migration (DVM), a flexible bioenergetics engine with three taxon-specific metabolic architectures, and a spatial pathfinding algorithm for habitat-seeking movement. These processes are made computationally tractable through a hybrid CPU/GPU architecture written in Julia. Unlike prior ABMs that use fixed perception radii or single-equation bioenergetics, SwimmingIndividuals enables emergent ecological dynamics across populations of superindividual agents. We demonstrate the framework through three Gulf of America simulations. The model reproduced DVM, cetacean diving patterns, size-based trophic shifts, and von Bertalanffy growth curves consistent with published King Mackerel stock assessments. Long-term simulations generated realistic population dynamics and quantified impacts of fishery harvest control rules across five fleets. SwimmingIndividuals provides a powerful, flexible, open-source platform for mechanistic marine ecosystem research and ecosystem-based fisheries management.

    Sperm phospholipase C signaling via PLCζ1 and PLCδ4 is essential for Ca2+ oscillations and fertility in mice

    by Neha Gupta, Lei Zhang, Hiroki Akizawa, Maria G. Gervasi, Darya A. Tourzani, Pablo E. Visconti, Ana Romarowski, Mariano Buffone, Wei Cui, Rafael A. Fissore

    Fertilization-induced calcium (Ca2+) oscillations are essential for egg activation and embryonic development in mammals. The sperm-specific phospholipase C isoform, PLCζ1, is the primary trigger of these oscillations. Functional and clinical studies have linked mutations or reduced expression of PLCζ1 to male infertility. However, whether PLCζ1 alone accounts for Ca2+ release at fertilization in all mammals remains uncertain. Male mice lacking Plcζ1 exhibit subfertility and reduced litter sizes; their sperm induce delayed, low-frequency Ca2+ oscillations, indicating a compensatory mechanism. Notably, mouse sperm express high levels of PLCδ4, essential for completing the acrosome reaction (AR). In Plcζ1-null sperm, PLCδ4 is upregulated and retained post-AR. These observations suggest a secondary functional role for PLCδ4. Using CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing, we generated Plcδ4 knockout (KO) mice. Plcδ4-null sperm trigger attenuated, delayed Ca2+ oscillations and significantly reduced fertilization rates, indicating that PLCδ4 contributes to but does not independently drive Ca2+ signaling. We therefore generated a Double Knockout (DKO) mouse line lacking both sperm-enriched PLCs. DKO males were sterile, with sperm failing to induce Ca2+ oscillations, and the isolated residual Ca2+ rise was insufficient to activate eggs. These findings reveal that PLCζ1, the primary oscillator, and PLCδ4, a compensatory factor, work cooperatively within a complex, multi-component PLC system that ensures robust fertilization. This work reveals molecular specialization among mammals in initiating the fertilization Ca2+ signals. It has implications for understanding male infertility, improving assisted reproductive technologies, and developing contraceptive strategies.

    Motor Neurons in the Prothoracic Ganglion of the Praying Mantis, Tenodera sinensis

    For understanding the neural mechanisms underlying the motor control, we comprehensively stained potential motor neurons in the prothoracic ganglion of the praying mantis by dye filling from each lateral nerve (PN1–5). We classified them into 13 clusters on the basis of the position of primary neurites relative to neuropils.


    ABSTRACT

    To understand the neural mechanisms underlying a behavior, it is important to identify the motor neurons innervating the muscles involved in the target behavior. In this study, we comprehensively stained potential motor neurons in the prothoracic ganglion of the praying mantis (Tenodera sinensis Saussure) by dye filling from each lateral nerve. We registered potential motor neurons into the standard atlas of the prothoracic ganglion and classified them into 13 clusters on the basis of the position of primary neurites relative to neuropils. Cell bodies of most motor neurons clustered in the anterior region of the hemiganglion ipsilateral to the filled nerve. Two clusters were located in the posteroventral region and the dorsocentral region. The latter cluster is likely to be a group of dorsal unpaired median neurons. Other neurons showed morphological features specific to common inhibitor neurons and sensory neurons. Most sensory afferents terminated in the anterior, ventralmost, and lateral ventral association center. These results provide fundamental knowledge that is helpful for understanding the neural mechanisms underlying the motor control of foreleg movements in the mantis.

    Teacher-Mediated, Not Learner-Facing: A Bayesian Quasi-Experimental Study of Agentic AI Support in Grade 10 Functions and Graphs [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into school mathematics, yet most research has focused on learner-facing systems rather than AI that supports teachers during instruction. This study examined whether teacher-mediated agentic AI could improve Grade 10 learners’ achievement in functions and graphs while preserving teachers’ central instructional role. Methods A quasi-experimental study was conducted with 115 Grade 10 learners from two intact classes following the same eight-week instructional programme. One class received teacher-mediated AI-supported instruction, while the comparison class received conventional instruction. Bayesian baseline-adjusted models estimated differences in post-test achievement, mastery-threshold probabilities, and variation across baseline achievement levels. Learners’ mathematical interest and confidence were analysed as secondary outcomes. Results The Bayesian model estimated a baseline-adjusted advantage of 29.12 points for the AI-supported condition (95% credible interval: 27.31–30.92). Posterior estimates indicated substantially higher probabilities of reaching achievement thresholds of 70, 80, 90, and 95 under teacher-mediated AI-supported instruction than under conventional instruction. The estimated achievement contrast remained positive across the observed baseline achievement distribution. Learners in the AI-supported condition also demonstrated higher post-intervention interest and confidence than those receiving conventional instruction. Because the study employed two intact classes taught by different teachers, the findings should be interpreted cautiously as evidence from a quasi-experimental design rather than definitive causal effects. Conclusions Teacher-mediated agentic AI represents a distinct instructional configuration in which AI extends teachers’ pedagogical capacity without replacing professional judgement. Although the findings suggest considerable potential for supporting mathematics teaching, larger multi-teacher studies are needed to determine the robustness and generalisability of these effects.

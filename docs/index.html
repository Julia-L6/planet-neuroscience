    Teacher-Mediated, Not Learner-Facing: A Bayesian Quasi-Experimental Study of Agentic AI Support in Grade 10 Functions and Graphs [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly being integrated into school mathematics, yet most research has focused on learner-facing systems rather than AI that supports teachers during instruction. This study examined whether teacher-mediated agentic AI could improve Grade 10 learners’ achievement in functions and graphs while preserving teachers’ central instructional role. Methods A quasi-experimental study was conducted with 115 Grade 10 learners from two intact classes following the same eight-week instructional programme. One class received teacher-mediated AI-supported instruction, while the comparison class received conventional instruction. Bayesian baseline-adjusted models estimated differences in post-test achievement, mastery-threshold probabilities, and variation across baseline achievement levels. Learners’ mathematical interest and confidence were analysed as secondary outcomes. Results The Bayesian model estimated a baseline-adjusted advantage of 29.12 points for the AI-supported condition (95% credible interval: 27.31–30.92). Posterior estimates indicated substantially higher probabilities of reaching achievement thresholds of 70, 80, 90, and 95 under teacher-mediated AI-supported instruction than under conventional instruction. The estimated achievement contrast remained positive across the observed baseline achievement distribution. Learners in the AI-supported condition also demonstrated higher post-intervention interest and confidence than those receiving conventional instruction. Because the study employed two intact classes taught by different teachers, the findings should be interpreted cautiously as evidence from a quasi-experimental design rather than definitive causal effects. Conclusions Teacher-mediated agentic AI represents a distinct instructional configuration in which AI extends teachers’ pedagogical capacity without replacing professional judgement. Although the findings suggest considerable potential for supporting mathematics teaching, larger multi-teacher studies are needed to determine the robustness and generalisability of these effects.

In this paper I propose that a close homolog of that kinase, fructosamine-3-phosphate-related protein, phosphorylates 3-ketosamine, a Maillard intermediate downstream from fructosamine, to 3-ketosamine-4-phosphate.     Cybersecurity Disclosure and Financial Performance: The Moderating Role of Board Characteristics [version 3; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Cybersecurity has become a strategic governance issue that affects firms’ financial performance and long-term resilience. While cybersecurity disclosure may enhance stakeholder confidence by improving transparency, limited evidence exists on whether board characteristics influence the relationship between cybersecurity disclosure and financial performance. This study examines the impact of cybersecurity disclosure on firm financial performance and investigates the moderating roles of board gender diversity and technological expertise in the Saudi Arabian context. Methods The study uses a balanced panel of 155 non-financial firms listed on the Saudi Exchange (Tadawul) over the period 2020–2024. Pooled ordinary least squares (OLS) regression is employed as the baseline estimation method, while System Generalized Method of Moments (System GMM) is used to address potential endogeneity concerns. Additional robustness analyses are conducted using alternative measures of cybersecurity disclosure and financial performance. Results The findings indicate that cybersecurity disclosure is positively associated with firm financial performance, suggesting that greater transparency regarding cybersecurity practices contributes to improved organizational outcomes. In contrast, cybersecurity incident disclosure is negatively associated with financial performance, reflecting the adverse consequences of reported cyber incidents. Furthermore, board gender diversity and board technological expertise significantly strengthen the positive relationship between cybersecurity disclosure and financial performance. The results remain robust across alternative model specifications, disclosure measures, and dynamic estimation techniques. Conclusions The findings demonstrate that the financial benefits of cybersecurity disclosure depend not only on the extent of disclosure but also on the quality of corporate governance. Boards with greater gender diversity and technological expertise enhance firms’ ability to translate cybersecurity transparency into superior financial performance. These findings provide practical implications for regulators, corporate boards, and policymakers seeking to strengthen cybersecurity governance, improve disclosure practices, and enhance organizational resilience in support of Saudi Vision 2030.

    Same Hospital, Same Tariff, Different Protection: Out-of-pocket Expenditure among Road Traffic Accident Admissions under Public Insurance, Private Insurance and Self-payment in India [version 2; peer review: 2 approved with reservations]

    Background Out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE) finances 43.4% of total health expenditure in India, and emergency trauma—unforeseeable, unpostponable and costly—is among the sharpest tests of whether a health-financing mechanism protects households from catastrophic spending. Insured and uninsured patients are normally treated in different facilities at different tariffs, confounding the effect of the payment mechanism with the setting in which care is given. Methods In this retrospective cross-sectional study, we compared OOPE across three financing arrangements—Ayushman Bharat–Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB-PMJAY), private health insurance and self-payment—using billing records for 1,599 of 1,711 consecutive road traffic accident admissions to a tertiary trauma centre in coastal Karnataka, India (July 2023–July 2024). All groups were billed under the same tariff. Charges were compared with and without adjustment for age, department and road-user category. OOPE was the patient payment recorded in hospital billing. Results Of 1,599 patients, 63 (3.9%) were AB-PMJAY beneficiaries, 262 (16.4%) were privately insured and 1,274 (79.7%) were uninsured. Median total charges were similar (₹90,979, ₹86,170 and ₹85,091; Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.70), including after adjustment (ratio of medians against the uninsured 0.95, 95% CI 0.85–1.08, for AB-PMJAY; 1.02, 0.89–1.14, for private insurance). No AB-PMJAY beneficiary had any recorded hospital OOPE. Privately insured patients paid a median of ₹11,598 (mean 25.6% of charges), highest under the community mutual-aid scheme (mean 36.4% of charges). Uninsured patients paid the entire charge. OOPE exceeded 10% of per-capita income for 0%, 22.5% and 78.7% of the three groups. Conclusions Recorded OOPE followed payer status while charges did not. Injury severity and household means were unobserved, so this is an association, not a causal effect. Four-fifths of admissions had no cover registered with the hospital. India’s 2025 cashless trauma entitlement addresses that gap, but 23.7% of uninsured admissions here were billed above its ₹1.5 lakh ceiling.

    Empowering the Community-based Bamboo Utilization to Advance Sustainable Coastal Ecosystem in East Lampung Regency [version 3; peer review: 3 approved with reservations]

    Abstract* Background Coastal ecosystems, including mangrove forests, are at the core of the socio-economic life of the local communities. Conversely, these ecosystems remain highly sensitive to the increasing impacts of climate change. Against such a backdrop, bamboo has been an important material, especially for construction and tool-making in coastal areas like East Lampung Regency. Despite the importance, such bamboo demands have yet to be met by local supplies or by the development of downstream value-added processing. Policy and Implications This study illustrates the strategic potential of bamboo as an economic and ecological resource, contributing to coastal resilience in East Lampung. If integrated into the Nature-based Solutions (NbS) framework, bamboo utilization can enhance livelihoods while restoring and protecting coastal ecosystems in communities. Conclusions Bamboo utilization in East Lampung has the potential to change from a traditional subsistence material to a strategic instrument toward coastal sustainability. Bamboo-based initiatives can support ecological resilience, help boost the economy along the coast, and inspire a stronger socio-cultural regeneration toward a more sustainable future for East Lampung through the NbS approach.

    Diversity and Functionality of the Gut Microbiota in Hypertensive Patients from Different Regions of Peru [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Introduction Hypertension affects approximately 14.2% of people over 15 years of age in Peru, constituting a major public health issue. Recent evidence highlights the gut microbiome’s role in regulating blood pressure through bioactive metabolites that influence inflammation and metabolism. This study aims to characterize the gut microbiota of hypertensive adults from three Peruvian regions, contributing to the understanding of microbial influences on hypertension. Objective To analyze the composition and functionality of the gut microbiota in hypertensive and normotensive adults from different Peruvian regions, considering age, body mass index (BMI), and geographical location. Methods An observational, cross-sectional, and analytical study was conducted with 84 adults (46 hypertensive and 38 normotensive) from nine regions of Peru. Fecal samples were collected for 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing of the V3–V4 region. Alpha and beta diversity were evaluated using the Shannon index, UniFrac distances, and multivariate analyses. Microbial functional pathways were examined to identify potential metabolic differences between groups. Results The dominant phyla in all groups were Bacillota and Bacteroidota. At the genus level, Streptococcus was associated with hypertensive patients with BMI ≥25, while Alistipes appeared only in normotensive individuals with BMI <25. Alpha diversity was greater in hypertensive participants under 50 years and with BMI <25, while lower diversity was seen in normotensive adults over 50 and with BMI ≥25. Beta diversity analyses showed substantial overlap across hypertensive status, age, and regional groups, with no clear separation. Adjusted multivariable models confirmed no significant independent association between hypertension status and either alpha or beta diversity after accounting for age, BMI, and region, with region remaining the strongest independent correlate of community composition (R2 = 16.3%, p = 0.001). Functional profiles were similar among groups, mainly involving carbohydrate, amino acid, and lipid metabolism, though BMI and age modulated these patterns. Conclusions Gut microbiota composition and diversity in this Peruvian cohort were associated not only with hypertension status but also with anthropometric and geographic factors. Despite similar functional profiles between groups, environmental and nutritional context appeared to have a greater influence than hypertension status alone. These exploratory, hypothesis-generating findings should be confirmed in larger, adjusted, longitudinal studies before informing preventive or therapeutic strategies for local settings.

    Financial literacy and tax management among market traders in northern Peru: the moderating role of business formalisation [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background In Peru, 41% of adults do not reach a minimum level of financial literacy, and informal employment exceeds 86% in Cajamarca and Amazonas. Little is known about whether market traders’ financial knowledge shows in how they handle their taxes, or about the conditions that change this relationship. This study estimated it in two markets in northern Peru, with accounting digitalisation, business formalisation and digital financial transformation as moderators. Methods Quantitative, observational, cross-sectional study with a non-probability sample of 218 traders from the markets of Chota (n = 109) and Bagua Grande (n = 109) in 2026. A 36-item Likert questionnaire, validated by expert judgement, was used, with Cronbach’s alpha between 0.927 and 0.977. Twenty-one linear regressions with interaction terms and centred predictors were estimated, with HC3 standard errors and bootstrap intervals from 5000 resamples for each interaction. Results Low financial literacy was found in 28.0% of traders and low tax management in 33.5%. Financial literacy had a positive partial effect on tax management (b between 0.446 and 0.519, p < 0.001), and only business formalisation moderated this effect (b = 0.053, 95% CI 0.008 to 0.096). The three dimensions of financial literacy were associated with tax management without moderation, and formalisation strengthened the effect on digital tax compliance (b = 0.071, 95% CI 0.011 to 0.130), which held without influential cases. Conclusions Financial literacy goes hand in hand with better tax management, above all in formalised businesses. Common method bias and the closeness between constructs mean these coefficients should be confirmed with data from other sources, such as tax administration records.

    Clinical, biochemical, radiological and histopathological profile of cholestasis in infancy: a single-centre retrospective cohort study of 101 infants from South India [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Cholestasis in infancy carries a heterogeneous aetiology. Extrahepatic biliary atresia and intrahepatic disorders share a clinical picture of persistent jaundice, yet outcomes in extrahepatic disease deteriorate rapidly with each week of diagnostic delay. Contemporary Indian data on their separation using an integrated diagnostic approach are limited. Methods We conducted a single-centre retrospective cohort study of infants (birth to one year) with prolonged cholestatic jaundice at a tertiary hospital in South India between January 2017 and December 2023. Each infant was classified as extrahepatic or intrahepatic on the basis of scintigraphy, ultrasonography, liver biopsy and intra-operative cholangiography. Continuous variables were compared with the Mann Whitney U test (effect size r reported), and receiver operating characteristic analysis was performed for candidate biochemical predictors. Results Of 101 infants, 61 (60.4 per cent) had extrahepatic cholestasis and 40 (39.6 per cent) had intrahepatic cholestasis. Median direct bilirubin was 8.94 versus 5.70 milligrams per decilitre (p equals 0.001, r equals 0.35), gamma glutamyl transferase 526 versus 128 units per litre (p less than 0.001, r equals 0.41), and total protein 6.10 versus 5.05 grams per decilitre (p less than 0.001, r equals 0.47). Total protein yielded the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (0.79, 95 per cent confidence interval 0.67 to 0.90; Youden cut off 5.8, sensitivity 75.5 per cent, specificity 76.0 per cent). Mortality was 34.7 per cent versus 14.3 per cent (risk difference 20.4 per cent, 95 per cent confidence interval 1.8 to 39.0). Conclusions Extrahepatic biliary atresia dominated referrals and diagnosis was often delayed. Total protein, direct bilirubin and gamma glutamyl transferase discriminated the two groups with clinically useful accuracy. A tiered diagnostic pathway anchored on these biochemistry markers is warranted for early identification of extrahepatic biliary atresia in Indian tertiary settings.

    Pharmaceutical Management Responses, Medication Safety and Patient Outcomes during Medicine Shortages and Stock-outs in Health Facilities: A Systematic Review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background When a medicine is missing, facilities substitute, conserve or seek other supplies, changing what reaches the bedside and the patient. This evidence is divided between pharmacy, nursing and outcome studies and between income settings. This review synthesized studies relating responses to medication-safety consequences and patient outcomes. Methods Following PRISMA 2020, Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE and OpenAlex were searched on 7 October 2026, and citations of included studies were searched. Eligible studies were English-language journal articles from 2020 with empirical data on at least two of response, consequence and outcome. Two authors independently screened 1,476 records and assessed 170 full texts, plus 23 from citation searching. Appraisal used MMAT 2018, synthesis followed SWiM with effect-direction coding, and confidence was rated with GRADE-CERQual. Results We included 142 studies (143 reports) from 69 countries; 110 (77.5%) were from high-income countries and 123 (86.6%) from hospitals. Therapeutic substitution was the most frequent response (85 studies; high confidence). In measured hospital comparisons, mortality, length of stay and readmission mostly showed no clear difference (moderate confidence), whereas worse disease control was reported mainly when long-term treatment was interrupted or switched (moderate confidence). The share of respondents associating shortages with medication errors ranged from 1.1% to 77.7%, and four studies measured errors (moderate confidence). In low- and middle-income settings, staff and patients reported referral to buy medicines elsewhere (moderate confidence). Procurement, inventory and governance responses were least often connected to any consequence or outcome (high confidence). No study reported occupational exposure or outcomes of nutrition commodity stock-outs. Conclusions Evidence is densest for clinical decisions in high-income hospitals and thinnest for supply and governance responses and for primary care in low- and middle-income countries. Substitutes without a close therapeutic equivalent and interrupted long-term treatment warrant monitoring, and studies with concurrent controls and measured errors and outcomes are needed.

    Attribution clause as a tool for addressing contractual imbalances and applicability in civil contracts under Jordanian law, with reference to French and Egyptian law [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Act Long-term contracts with monetary obligations are exposed to changes in economic circumstances, yet the Jordanian Civil Law contains no specific provision on the clause by which the parties agree in advance that such obligations will be adjusted automatically. This article examines the attribution clause (also called the indexation clause) as a mechanism for preserving the contractual balance, and asks under what conditions the clause is valid and effective under Jordanian law, in comparison with French and Egyptian law. The study is qualitative: it uses doctrinal and comparative legal analysis of legislation, judicial decisions and scholarly writing. The attribution clause acts as a tool to modify the terms of a contract by accepting pre-specified terms and conditions. This methodology ensures that the contractual relationship is protected while addressing potentially complex scenarios that may arise during contract execution due to evolving circumstances. To maintain the binding force of the contract, Parties expedite dispute resolution and protect the integrity of their contracts by choosing recourse and prior agreeing to remedies, which encourages continuity in enforcement. These clauses are intended to automatically amend the terms of the contract without requiring the parties to participate in the amendment process. These items adjust the amount of funds subject to the obligation according to changes observed by external indicators associated with the obligation. This is done by comparing the price of a particular currency with the price of a different commodity. In the case of payment in gold or foreign currency, it represents an assault on the binding legal force of the national currency by competing with it as a means of payment and considering it as one of them. Terms of payment in gold and foreign currency are excluded from the attribution requirement, which replaces legal means of payment with new means of payment. It does not constitute a basis for determining the extent of fulfillment of the obligation, and we conclude that the attribution clause is a distinct legal system that deals with amending contractual terms and the contractual imbalance they cause. Amendment is permitted in the event of circumstances occurring that would upset the balance throughout the contract implementation period, and we recommend adopting the attribution clause as an automatic adjustment tool and propose a legislative provision to regulate it. The clause is suited to contracts whose monetary obligations are performed over time (such as leases, loans, construction and supply contracts); it is not a remedy for breach and it is not claimed to apply to every civil contract.

    Analysis of the radionuclides and heavy metals concentrations of the Vaal River, South Africa. [version 2; peer review: 2 not approved]

    Background The Vaal River is a key freshwater source in South Africa, providing water for homes, farms, industry, and local ecosystems. However, urban growth, industry, and mining in the area have put pressure on the river. These activities have released radionuclides and heavy metals into the water. Methods ICP-MS (Elmer NexION, 2000) was used to analyse radionuclides and heavy metals in 27 water samples collected from the Vaal River in June 2023. The analysis was mainly for Li, B, Pb, Cu, Co, Ni, Zn, Mn, Fe, and P radionuclides, such as K, Th, and U. Results A health risk assessment of the Vaal River identified hazardous levels of heavy metals through, with children at significantly higher risk than adults. Cobalt presented the greatest risk to children (HQ = 169.57 mSv/y), while lithium posed risks to both adults (2.79 mSv/y) and children (27.97 mSv/y). Nickel, copper, and lead also threatened children’s health but not adults. Nickel accounted for 99.8% of total cancer risk, with overall cancer risks surpassing the acceptable limit of 1 × 10−6.The hazard index (HI) exceeded the safe threshold of 1 mSv/y for both adults (20.47) and children (205.09). Radionuclide levels for thorium (Th) and uranium (U) were within international safety limits. Radium equivalent activity was 33,803 Bq/l, absorbed dose rate was 18,158 nGy/h, annual effective dose was 0.0222 nGy/h, and hazard indices (Hex = 0.091, Hin = 0.096) were below UNSCEAR and IAEA thresholds. The strong links between radiological measures indicate low external exposure risk. The study recommends urgent monitoring and public health actions, especially to protect children, and calls for ongoing checks near industrial areas. Conclusions Heavy metal contamination in the Vaal River poses an urgent public health threat, with children most vulnerable; hazard indices and cancer risk far exceed safe limits, driven primarily by nickel. Radiological hazards from natural radionuclides remain within safe limits, however, constant monitoring is required. The study concludes that immediate regulatory interventions and heavy-metal pollution-control measures are needed, alongside continued monitoring of both contaminants, especially near industrial areas, to prevent further deterioration.

    Skilled women’s employment continuity and career re-entry in India: A structured review of conditions and employer support [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Higher female labour force participation does not necessarily indicate that qualified women obtain or sustain suitable paid employment. This review examines how care responsibilities, labour demand and workplace practices influence skilled women’s employment continuity and career re-entry in India. Methods A structured narrative review examined scholarly and institutional sources identified through targeted searches of publisher and institutional websites. Journal research was considered alongside official statistics and institutional reports. Evidence was compared by population, study design, observation period and employment outcome. Findings were synthesised narratively without statistical pooling. Results The evidence distinguishes aggregate participation from employment among qualified women, and organisational policy provision from demonstrated employment outcomes. Official statistics indicate that 51.4% of women aged 15–59 with formal vocational or technical training were employed; this is an employment measure rather than a labour force participation rate. Indian studies describe care constraints, occupational stereotypes, workplace support and varied experiences of career re-entry. Employer reports document organisational initiatives but do not establish sustained retention. Existing reviews examine reintegration, mentoring and organisational context. A key synthesis question concerns whether access to employer support translates into skill utilisation, earnings, employment continuity and career progression. Differences in populations, measures and study designs limit comparisons and causal interpretation. Conclusions Evaluating skilled women’s employment requires attention to outcomes beyond qualifications, policy availability and initial recruitment. Longitudinal and comparative research in India is needed to assess how employer support relates to durable employment outcomes valued by women, while accounting for care responsibilities and occupational settings.

    No Support Beyond Reach: The Measure Centrality Index 2.0 and Adversarial Comparisons of Consciousness Theories

    arXiv:2611.06925v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: A success in adversarial testing of scientific theories reaches only as deep as its matching failure would have cut. I call this the Reach Principle, and the goal of this chapter is to build the instrument that enforces it. Adversarial tests of consciousness theories can turn into a one-way evidential ratchet. When a preregistered prediction passes, the success is claimed for the research program; when it fails, the failure lands on an auxiliary involved in measurement or implementation. The ratchet turns wherever attribution is settled after the outcome is known. Against it, I define a preregisterable test object, putting the attribution policy on the record before the result, comprising what is compared, under which assumptions, when data suffice to settle an outcome, and which targets of a dated theory version each outcome exposes. The Measure Centrality Index 2.0 extends the original version to the positive-evidence side, recording for every possible outcome what it would count against and what its proponent has agreed to change. I run the framework on Cogitate's third prediction, where the preregistered target was the posterior-connectivity implementation and the mathematical or computational core of both Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory stood outside the test. The record shows that a pass could have been claimed at any depth, because nothing fenced success the way the failure was bound. The next preregistration can set reach in advance, and support for a theory then reaches only what was exposed ex ante.

    Foundation Models as Modular Tools for fMRI Analysis

    arXiv:2611.06926v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Foundation models (FMs) for the brain are rapidly growing in number and each captures a different aspect of the same data. A single scientific question often needs several of them at once, alongside classical analysis and the literature, but no established method combines them, because each model defines its own inputs, outputs, and spatial conventions. Representation alignment, the usual route from an FM into a language model, binds one FM to one LLM and requires retraining for every model added. We take a modular route: each FM becomes an independent tool server that a fixed language agent calls next to classical and literature tools, with no retraining. We start with fMRI, where public FMs already disagree on how to name a brain location and open task data give a classical baseline, and serve two FMs at opposite ends of that spectrum, BrainLM (atlas parcels) and Omni-fMRI (atlas-free voxels). A 76-question benchmark, a 2 by 2 tool ablation, and an independent LLM judge yield 3,040 judged traces.

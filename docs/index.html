    Asymptotic Solution of Fermats Last Theorem Using Trigonometric Analysis [version 3; peer review: 1 approved]

    This manuscript develops an asymptotic solution of the trigonometric system induced by Fermat's Last Theorem1. A hypothetical positive solution of xn + yn = zn determines an acute triangle and an associated right triangle, yielding tan2θ = (sin α/sin β)n. Fixed nonoscillatory roots collapse to zero, while regular interior accumulation for general fixed frequencies is confined to the level set sin (at)/sin (bt) = 1. For normalized Fermat angles, the unit-frequency region b ≤ 1 is geometrically impossible. On the ordered acute branch, the fast and slow regimes and the boundary layer are classified using a uniform Taylor remainder and the joint cluster set. The global multiplicity of critical roots remains open. Lean 4 verifies the fixed-frequency results, root bounds, the nonempty admissible interval, the exact boundary cosine reduction, the unit-frequency obstruction, and endpoint limit transfers.

    Closing the Gap in Early Mathematics: Domain and Cognitive Insights from TIMSS 2023 in South Africa and Singapore [version 5; peer review: 1 approved, 2 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background South Africa continues to underperform in primary mathematics, yet overall mean comparisons obscure the distribution of achievement differences across mathematics content and cognitive domains. This study examines domain-specific patterns of mathematics achievement using data from the Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS) 2023, benchmarking South African Grade 5 learners against Singaporean Grade 4 learners assessed on the same TIMSS Grade 4 mathematics framework. Methods A quantitative secondary analysis was conducted using nationally representative TIMSS 2023 datasets comprising 10,424 South African learners from 285 schools and 6,530 Singaporean learners from 181 schools. South Africa assessed Grade 5 learners using the TIMSS Grade 4 mathematics instruments, reflecting its approach of assessing learners at a higher grade to achieve a closer match between TIMSS content and national curriculum coverage. Mathematics achievement was analysed across content domains (Number, Measurement and Geometry, Data) and cognitive domains (Knowing, Applying, Reasoning). Weighted estimates, replicate-based variance estimation, and effect sizes (Cohen’s .) were computed in accordance with IEA technical guidelines. The analysis is descriptive and comparative rather than causal. Results South African learners performed substantially below the international centre point across all domains. The largest content-domain gap relative to Singapore was observed in Measurement and Geometry, while the largest cognitive-domain gap occurred in Knowing. Although Applying was relatively stronger within the South African profile, it remained substantially below benchmark levels. Substantial achievement differences were evident across all content and cognitive domains, with the largest descriptive differences observed in Measurement and Geometry and Knowing. Conclusions Substantial achievement differences were evident across all mathematics content and cognitive domains, with comparatively larger differences observed in Measurement and Geometry and Knowing. The results provide a structured diagnostic profile of domain-specific achievement disparities, highlighting variation across mathematics content and cognitive domains.

    Developing an AI Model to Identify and Support Women at Risk of Postpartum Depression in Pakistan: A Longitudinal Study Protocol [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Postpartum depression (PPD) is a major global health concern, particularly in low- and middle-income countries such as Pakistan, where prevalence ranges from 28%–63%. PPD affects maternal well-being and mother–child bonding. Perinatal mental health services are limited by stigma, cultural barriers, and a lack of care. Current approaches rely primarily on symptom-based screening and reactive treatment. Therefore, the study aims to develop and validate an AI-based model and digital application to identify pregnant women at risk of perinatal anxiety and PPD and to facilitate timely intervention. Methods This study adopts a three-phase translational research design to develop, validate, and implement an AI-enabled digital application, Sehatmand Maa (Healthy Mother), for early identification and management of PPD in Pakistan. Phase 1, a prospective longitudinal cohort already underway, will enroll 1,581 pregnant women from three Maternal and Child Health facilities in Karachi between April 2024 and December 2026. Participants will be assessed at four time points: two during the prenatal period (T0 and T1) and two during the postnatal period (T3 and T4), using clinical and psychosocial measures, including EPDS, PSAS, PSQI, and MSPSS. Phase 2, currently in progress, uses machine-learning algorithms, including CatBoost, to develop and validate separate predictive models for perinatal anxiety and PPD. Phase 3, not yet started, will integrate validated models into Sehatmand Maa and pilot the application across the three facilities to assess feasibility, usability, and integration into routine maternal healthcare. Expected Results The study will generate context-specific evidence on predictors of perinatal anxiety and PPD and establish validated AI-based models for early risk identification. The application is expected to support risk stratification, clinical decision-making, and timely referral of high-risk women. Pilot findings will inform refinement before broader implementation, with the potential to provide a scalable, AI-enabled Tool for maternal mental health care in low-resource settings.

    Using Corpus-Based Approaches to Teach Arabic as a Foreign Language: A Sketch Engine–Driven Model [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Corpus-based Arabic language teaching is increasingly urgent as digital learning demands authentic, reproducible evidence of usage. Yet Arabic as a foreign language remains dominated by decontextualized vocabulary and prescriptive rules. This study addresses the underexplored pedagogical integration of Sketch Engine into Arabic vocabulary instruction through naturally occurring discourse. Methods Guided by data-driven learning and corpus linguistics, particularly the principles of Johns, Sinclair, McEnery, and Boulton, the study employs a qualitative corpus-based design. Future research should experimentally evaluate proficiency gains, develop level-sensitive corpus tasks, construct multilingual learner corpora, and openly share datasets and teaching workflows to ensure replication and cross-contextual adaptation.

    Assessing the accuracy and patient acceptability of AI vs human translation of patient-facing clinical trial documentation: A mixed methods comparison study [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Diversity in research participation is essential to understand the generalisability of trial findings and reduce health inequalities. Inability to understand English is an exclusion criterion for many trials, where recruitment and consent processes rely on written materials. Professional translation is resource heavy while AI offers quick, free translation but evidence for its accuracy and accessibility is lacking, particularly where complex information needs to be conveyed clearly. We compared AI to professional human translations to assess whether these could be a viable alternative in clinical research. Methods A selection of trial documents were translated into three of the most common non-English or Welsh languages in the UK (Polish, Punjabi, Chinese) by professional human translators and widely available AI applications, and reverse translated back to English. We conducted a dual-layered evaluation: i) End-User Acceptability: Native speakers evaluated translated documents, providing feedback through surveys and interviews; ii) Linguistic Coding Analysis: Independent reviewers compared reverse-translations against original English texts with findings validated by a sixth reviewer. Reviewers were blinded to which were AI or professional translations. Results While human translation is generally more accurate than AI, error margins are small and AI translations were found to be an acceptable alternative by native language reviewers. AI applications varied in performance, with Claude outperforming other applications. Translations into European language (Polish) were generally more accurate, though there was little variation across document type. Errors in medical and regulatory terminology showed the need for human validation across all translation pathways. Conclusions Translations by AI applications are generally accurate enough as a support mechanism to clinical trial recruitment, but careful human oversight is required, particularly for consent processes. Further evaluation of AI translations in real-world trial settings is needed to provide robust evidence of their acceptability to trial participants and develop best practice guidance for trial teams.

    Objective To examine the association between four dimensions of document digitization and staff perceptions regarding resource use, goal achievement, and the quality of spending in a Peruvian municipality. The assessment of potential cost reductions requires accounting records and administrative indicators measured over time.

    From Needs Assessment to Instructional Design: Empirical Requirements for a DIF-PBL Model in Inclusive Numeracy Learning [version 2; peer review: 3 approved with reservations]

    Inclusive elementary classrooms require instructional approaches that accommodate substantial variation in students’ numeracy readiness, learning characteristics, and support needs. This study examined teachers’ instructional needs and preliminary learner differences as an empirical basis for designing a Differentiation-Based Problem-Based Learning (DIF-PBL) model for inclusive numeracy learning. The study was conducted as a descriptive needs assessment within a broader research and development project. Participants included 20 classroom teachers and special education support teachers from three inclusive elementary schools in Bone Regency, South Sulawesi, Indonesia. Data were collected using a 13-item four-point Likert questionnaire and structured observations of three students representing different learner profiles. Questionnaire data were analyzed using item-level means, standard deviations, and response frequencies, while student observations were treated as illustrative contextual cases. Teachers reported substantial instructional challenges, including disparities in numeracy achievement, difficulty balancing instructional support, limitations in conventional Problem-Based Learning, and practical difficulties in implementing differentiated instruction. The findings support several design requirements, including differentiated learning pathways, flexible PBL procedures, Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract learning progression, systematic scaffolding, adaptive assessment, and practical teacher guidance. The findings provide an empirical starting point for subsequent prototype development and validation rather than evidence of model effectiveness.

    Editorial Discretion and Procedural Fairness: A Framework for Accountable Desk-Rejection Decisions [version 2; peer review: 1 approved with reservations]

    Desk rejection is a legitimate and necessary exercise of editorial discretion, but concerns arise when irrelevant author characteristics, rather than manuscript-level criteria or documented integrity risks, determine outcomes. This Opinion article examines the possible problem of author-based desk rejection, using prolific research activity in computational science and engineering as an illustrative—not evidentiary—case. It proposes seven principles: reason-giving, consistency, conflict management, reviewability, correctability, auditability and insulation from inappropriate metric pressure. The revised analysis emphasizes that these principles largely overlap with existing editorial standards; a new framework cannot by itself reveal concealed motives, eliminate good-faith disagreement among editors or guarantee compliance. Appeals and audits should be targeted, proportionate and adapted to journal governance, resources and disciplinary context, without creating an entitlement to peer review. Evidence on the prevalence of author-based desk rejection and the incremental effectiveness of formal safeguards remains insufficient. Finally, citation-driven pressures are shaped substantially by universities, funders and academic evaluation systems as well as publishers; this structural issue extends beyond the desk-rejection framework. The article presents a research and governance agenda for testing feasible safeguards while protecting editorial independence.

    Background Microgreen production in compact controlled environments requires timely irrigation and lighting, but many small-scale systems still rely on manual observation. Methods An iterative engineering design was applied.     Curriculum unlearning in humans and neural networks

    arXiv:2611.04565v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: During learning, humans often benefit from curricula that teach simple rules before exceptions. However, we lack a computational explanation for this phenomenon. Here, using a canonical behavioural task and modelling with neural networks, we develop and test a theory for why simple-to-complex curricula succeed. We show that during learning, both humans and neural networks show simplicity biases, a tendency to learn simple rules that are underfit to the data. In neural networks this bias is accompanied by compression of neural representations.

    Extraction of Oleoresin from Nutmeg (Myristica fragrans Houtt) Seed Distillation Waste Using the UAE (Ultrasound-Assisted Extraction) Method [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Nutmeg (Myristica fragrans Houtt.) seed distillation waste remains underutilized despite its potential as a source of valuable compounds. This study investigated its use as a raw material for oleoresin production using ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE). Methods Oleoresin was extracted using 96% food-grade ethanol at a material-to-solvent ratio of 1:4 (w/v). A factorial completely randomized design evaluated three temperatures (30, 40, and 50°C) and three extraction times (35, 45, and 55 min), with two replications per treatment. Yield was calculated, and the sample with the highest yield was characterized using gas chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (GC–MS/MS) and gas chromatography–flame ionization detection (GC–FID). Data were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA). Results Temperature significantly affected oleoresin yield (p = 0.024), whereas extraction time (p = 0.791) and the temperature–time interaction (p = 0.517) were not significant. The highest mean yield was 13.71 ± 1.75% at 50°C, while the highest individual yield was 14.91 ± 0.42% at 50°C for 45 min. Ten compounds were identified, with a neolignan compound (26.29%) and trans-isomyristicin (26.04%) showing the highest relative abundances. Saturated fatty acids accounted for 20.45%, mainly palmitic acid (12.94%) and myristic acid (5.46%). Conclusions Nutmeg seed distillation waste can be utilized for oleoresin production using UAE. Extraction at 50°C for 45 min produced the highest observed yield, while chemical and fatty acid profiles demonstrated the potential of the oleoresin as a source of valuable constituents.

    Efficacy and Safety of Rocatinlimab in Adults With Moderate‑to‑Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Rocatinlimab, an anti-OX40 monoclonal antibody, is a novel therapy for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD), a condition that significantly impairs quality of life. This meta-analysis evaluated its efficacy and safety. Methods Randomized controlled trials comparing rocatinlimab (150 mg and 300 mg) with placebo were included. Literature screening was performed using Zotero, and analyses were conducted with RevMan 5.4. Efficacy outcomes included EASI-75, EASI-90, vIGA-AD (0/1), pruritus NRS, and DLQI. Safety outcomes included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), serious adverse events, and specific side effects. Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% CIs were calculated. Results Rocatinlimab significantly improved EASI-75 (OR 3.93), EASI-90 (OR 4.78), and vIGA-AD 0/1 (OR 3.33) at week 24. Pruritus and quality of life also improved. The 150 mg dose showed benefit for EASI-75 (OR 3.95) and pruritus (OR 2.98), though other outcomes were inconsistent. TEAEs were comparable to placebo. The 300 mg dose was associated with more mild adverse effects (e.g., headache, pyrexia, aphthous ulcers) but fewer severe events. Conclusion Rocatinlimab demonstrates dose-dependent efficacy with an acceptable safety profile, with 300 mg offering greater clinical benefit.

    Nigella sativa-Based Extender Enhances Nilem (Osteochilus vittatus) Sperm Quality: Implications for Sustainable Aquaculture Practices [version 2; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations, 1 not approved]

    Background and Aim Preservation of Nilem (Osteochilus vittatus) spermatozoa is essential for sustainable aquaculture and the development of reproductive biotechnology. This study evaluated the effectiveness of a commercial habbatussauda (Nigella sativa) extract as a natural antioxidant-based extender for maintaining sperm quality during 48 h cold storage at 4 °C. Materials and Methods Sperm samples were supplemented with commercial habbatussauda extract at concentrations of 1% (P1), 2% (P2), 3% (P3), and 4% (P4). Untreated sperm served as a negative control (P0), while sperm treated with 0.005% pure thymoquinone acted as a positive control (PTQ). All samples were stored at 4 °C for 48 hours with six replicates per treatment. Post-storage assessments included sperm viability, motility, and morphological abnormalities. Ultrastructural integrity of spermatozoa was qualitatively evaluated using Cryogenic Scanning Electron Microscopy (CRYO-SEM). Results The 2% habbatussauda extract (P2) yielded the highest sperm viability (84.80 ± 5.54%) and motility (73.25 ± 10.82%) and significantly reduced abnormality rates compared to the untreated control. CRYO-SEM analysis revealed severe ultrastructural damage in untreated spermatozoa, including plasma membrane disruption, head deformation, and flagellar breakage. In contrast, spermatozoa preserved with 1–2% extract, particularly P2, maintained intact plasma membranes, normal head morphology, and well-preserved flagella. Higher extract concentrations (3–4%) induced mild ultrastructural alterations. Conclusion A 2% commercial habbatussauda (Nigella sativa) extract effectively preserves Nilem sperm quality and ultrastructure during short-term cold storage, supporting its application as a natural antioxidant protectant in aquaculture.

    Early Identification of Sepsis-induced Acute Kidney Injury Utilizing NGAL, Cystatin C, Urine Output, and Serum Creatinine in a Porcine (Sus scrofa) Model of Septic Shock: A comparative Study of Fluid and Vasopressor Strategies [version 2; peer review: 2 a

    Background Sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) contributes significantly to ICU morbidity and mortality, yet early detection remains challenging due to the delayed response of conventional markers such as serum creatinine and urine output. This study aimed to evaluate the temporal patterns of early renal biomarkers—neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) and cystatin C—compared with conventional functional markers in a porcine model of septic shock under three fluid and vasopressor resuscitation strategies. Methods In a randomized experimental study, six Sus scrofa pigs underwent standardized septic shock induction and were allocated into three resuscitation groups: Ringer’s lactate (30 mL/kg), fluid combined with early norepinephrine infusion, or early norepinephrine with maintenance fluids. Plasma NGAL, cystatin C, serum creatinine, and ultrasonographically assessed urine dynamics were measured sequentially alongside continuous hemodynamic monitoring. Data were analyzed using repeated-measures and nonparametric tests (p < 0.05). Results NGAL and cystatin C exhibited numerical temporal fluctuations across observation periods and differing trajectories among resuscitation strategies. Post-intervention decreases in NGAL and cystatin C coccurred in fluid-based groups, whereas early norepinephrine administration was followed by numerical increases. However, neither between-group nor within-group temporal changes reached statistical significance for NGAL, cystatin C, or serum creatinine. Conclusion In this exploratory pilot study, NGAL and cystatin C displayed numerical temporal shifts during septic shock; however, these changes lacked statistical significance and cannot establish early SA-AKI detection. Given the unstandardized urine output criteria and non-significant creatinine kinetics, these findings represent exploratory evidence of renal biomarker dynamics rather than definitive diagnostic proof of SA-AKI.

    Exploring Nurses’ Perceptions of the Relationships Among Nursing Resilience, Emotional Intelligence, Employee Engagement, and Work Efficiency: A Qualitative Study in Saudi Arabia [version 2; peer review: 1 not approved]

    Background Although emotional intelligence and nursing resilience have been associated with positive work outcomes, limited qualitative evidence has explored how nurses perceive the relationships among these psychological resources, employee engagement, and nursing work efficiency. This study explored nurses’ perceptions of the relationships among nursing resilience, emotional intelligence, employee engagement, and nursing work efficiency in a tertiary healthcare setting in Saudi Arabia. Methods A qualitative study using thematic analysis was conducted among 25 registered nurses at King Fahad Specialist Hospital, Qassim Health Cluster, Saudi Arabia. Participants were purposively recruited from intensive care, emergency, medical, surgical, and outpatient departments. Data were collected through 30–45-minute semi-structured in-depth interviews, supported by limited non-participant observations and document review. Interviews were conducted in Arabic or English according to participants’ language preference; interviews conducted in Arabic were translated into English for analysis. Interview transcripts were analysed using thematic analysis, with NVivo version 14 used to facilitate data management, coding, retrieval, and organization of themes. Trustworthiness was addressed through methodological triangulation and the criteria of Lincoln and Guba. Results Four themes emerged from the thematic analysis: emotional intelligence in managing clinical and interpersonal demands; nursing resilience in adapting to workplace pressures; employee engagement as a connecting experience in nursing practice; and perceived work efficiency in the context of psychological and workplace resources. Participants described emotional intelligence and nursing resilience as important resources for managing workplace challenges and maintaining professional functioning. Employee engagement was described in relation to motivation, commitment, collaboration, and participation in patient care. Participants also described work efficiency in terms of safe, organized, accurate, and high-quality nursing practice while managing demanding clinical responsibilities. Conclusions Participants described employee engagement as an important connecting experience within the relationships among emotional intelligence, nursing resilience, and nursing work efficiency. These findings reflect nurses’ perceptions and experiences rather than statistically tested mediation or causal relationships. The findings highlight the potential value of supportive leadership, resilience development, emotional intelligence development, psychological support, and strategies that foster employee engagement in supporting nurses’ work experiences and perceived work efficiency.

    Greenspace exposure and associated health outcomes: an updated systematic review of reviews [version 3; peer review: 5 approved with reservations]

    Background Studies, including umbrella reviews, have provided evidence linking greenspace exposure to specific health outcomes. To address methodological limitations in existing umbrella reviews, this umbrella review aims to expand knowledge on the association between greenspace and human health by summarising evidence from both quantitative and qualitative systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Methods This umbrella review builds upon our previous review with updates to reflect recent developments in the field. Five databases (PubMed, Embase, the Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature [CINAHL], Scopus, and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews) were searched for articles published in English in peer-reviewed journals between December 2020 and June 2024. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses were included if they clearly defined measures of greenspace exposure and reported health outcomes directly attributable to greenspace exposure (PROSPERO: CRD42022383421). The methodological quality and risk of bias of each included review were evaluated by two independent reviewers. Results A total of 45 articles were included in this umbrella review. Greenspace exposure was beneficially associated with mental health, general health and quality of life, and cardiovascular and metabolic health. Inconsistent or inconclusive associations were observed for respiratory health and allergies, maternal health and birth outcomes, cancer, and cognitive function. Some harmful associations were reported for communicable and vector-borne diseases, although these were based on a limited number of systematic reviews. Most of the included reviews had a high risk of bias and critically low methodological quality. Discussion This umbrella review highlights the multifaceted health benefits of greenspace exposure while underlining existing evidence gaps and methodological challenges. The results indicate that increasing access to greenspace could be a simple and cost-effective strategy to improve population health and reduce health inequalities.

    Menopausal Status, Estrogen Levels, and Hormone Replacement Therapy in Relation to Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo in Adult Women: A Protocol for a Systematic Review [version 1; peer review: awaiting peer review]

    Background Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is a common, recurrent cause of peripheral vertigo. Its incidence increases with age and is higher among women, particularly during and after the menopausal transition. Estrogen deficiency may affect calcium metabolism and otoconial stability, but available studies use heterogeneous definitions, measurements, and settings. Few systematic reviews have examined the relationship between menopause-related estrogen changes and BPPV. A focused, comprehensive synthesis of associations between menopausal status, estrogen levels, hormone replacement therapy, and BPPV is needed. Objective The aim of this planned systematic review is to systematically evaluate associations of menopausal status, serum estrogen or estradiol levels, and hormone replacement therapy with the occurrence, incidence, and recurrence of BPPV in adult women. Methods PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched for English-language studies published through 31 March 2026. Eligible quantitative studies will report associations of menopausal status, serum estrogen or estradiol concentrations, or hormone replacement therapy with BPPV occurrence, incidence, or recurrence in adult women. At least two reviewers will independently complete full-text selection, data extraction, and risk-of-bias assessment. Findings will be grouped by exposure and outcome and synthesized narratively in accordance with Synthesis Without Meta-analysis guidance. Certainty of evidence will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach. Conclusions The review will clarify the strength, consistency, and limitations of evidence linking menopausal and hormonal factors with BPPV. By recording the setting, diagnostic method, and case mix of each study, it will also make explicit how far this evidence can be transferred to routine clinical practice. It will not by itself establish an indication for routine hormone testing or hormone therapy but will identify evidence gaps and priorities for future research.     Risk Factors for Severe and Profound Sensorineural Hearing Loss and Auditory Performance in Children After Cochlear Implant Surgery in a Peruvian Pediatric Institute [version 3; peer review: 1 approved, 1 approved with reservations]

    Background Sensorineural hearing loss in children, when associated with specific risk factors, may impair language development and negatively affect quality of life, as well as family and social integration. Objective To determine the association between risk factors for severe-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss and auditory performance in post–cochlear implant patients in a Peruvian pediatric institute. Methods An analytical cross-sectional study was conducted using medical records of children who underwent cochlear implantation between 2021 and 2024. Sociodemographic and clinical data were collected, and auditory performance was assessed using the PEACH questionnaire. Poor auditory performance was defined as a PEACH score < 75%. Crude prevalence ratios were estimated through bivariate analysis. Multivariable associations were evaluated with logistic regression, reported as adjusted odds ratios (aOR), and with Poisson regression with robust variance, reported as adjusted prevalence ratios (aPR). Results Medical records from 100 children were analyzed. Multivariate analysis identified prematurity (aPR: 2.6; 95% CI: 1.5–4.5), low birth weight (aPR: 2.4; 95% CI: 1.4–4.2), and meningitis (aPR: 3.2; 95% CI: 1.5–7.0) as independent factors associated with poor auditory performance, with meningitis showing the strongest association. Severe perinatal asphyxia was also significantly associated with poorer outcomes (aPR: 2.2; 95% CI: 1.1–4.5). Age at implantation >3 years was significant in bivariate analysis but not after adjustment. Conclusions Neonatal risk factors, particularly prematurity, low birth weight, severe perinatal asphyxia, and meningitis, were significantly associated with poorer auditory performance after cochlear implantation in children with sensorineural hearing loss. Early identification and appropriate management of these risk factors, together with timely intervention and follow-up, may help optimize auditory outcomes in this population.

    Securing the Digital Edge: How Security Management Mediates the Impact of ICT Infrastructure on Firm Performance in Emerging Markets [version 3; peer review: 1 approved]

    Background Information and communication technology (ICT) infrastructure drives firm performance in emerging markets, yet institutional voids and security vulnerabilities often undermine its efficacy. The mechanisms linking ICT endowment, organizational security investment, and firm-level outcomes remain underexplored, particularly in developing economies. Methods We tested a recursive three-block structural equation model (SEM) on nationally representative microdata from Peru’s Annual Economic Survey 2024 (N = 9,966 firms across 14 sectors). The model integrated the Resource-Based View (RBV) and Routine Activity Theory (RAT) to specify pathways from ICT infrastructure, internet access, and digital human capital through security management investment to firm performance (large vs. medium/small firms, by INEI survey format). Parameters were estimated by maximum likelihood; robustness checks re-estimated the model without small firms and with continuous log sales as the outcome. Mediation analysis decomposed total effects via bootstrap confidence intervals (5,000 replications). Results ICT infrastructure was the dominant predictor across all equations (β = 0.357 in security management; β = 0.327 in firm performance, both p < 0.001). Security management significantly mediated 17.7% of ICT’s total effect on performance (indirect β = 0.070, p < 0.001). Criminal victimization exposure directly predicted security investment (β = 0.061, p < 0.001), establishing a protective response mechanism predicted by Routine Activity Theory. The main paths hold without small firms and with continuous log sales as the outcome. Model fit indices confirmed excellent fit (CFI = 0.982, RMSEA = 0.029, GFI = 0.980). Conclusions In volatile emerging market environments, security management functions as a strategic resource that transforms raw ICT connectivity into sustainable competitive advantage — a phenomenon we term “Digital Resilience.” These findings challenge technological determinism and provide a blueprint for firms and policymakers in developing economies to integrate security as a core dynamic capability rather than a cost center.

    A minimal E/I circuit model explains differential recovery from amblyopia under TTX and reverse occlusion

    arXiv:2611.06189v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Recovery from amblyopia beyond the critical period remains a major challenge in visual neuroscience. Experimental studies show that transient inactivation of the fellow eye with tetrodotoxin (TTX) produces stronger and more persistent recovery than reverse occlusion (RO), despite both interventions reducing competition from the fellow eye. The circuit mechanisms underlying this difference remain unclear. We develop a minimal cortical circuit model combining excitatory-inhibitory population dynamics, BCM-like metaplasticity, homeostatic regulation, and eye-specific synaptic plasticity. Monocular deprivation drives the network into an amblyopic operating regime with reduced excitatory activity and fellow-eye dominance. Phase-plane and operating-regime analyses show that TTX and RO subsequently place the circuit in distinct dynamical states. TTX lowers inhibitory activity while maintaining excitatory activity above the BCM modification threshold, creating a larger plasticity-permissive regime than RO. These differences produce stronger recovery of amblyopic-eye responsiveness and greater correction of ocular dominance under TTX. A local robustness analysis across 300 perturbed parameter sets shows that the TTX advantage is not dependent on a narrowly tuned parameter choice. These results provide a mechanistic account of the differential efficacy of fellow-eye inactivation and suggest that recovery from sensory deprivation depends not only on changes in sensory input, but also on the dynamical operating regime of the cortical circuit.

    Mind Control of LLMs via Closed-Loop Stimulation: Out-of-Distribution Robustness

    arXiv:2611.06418v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Brains and large language models need not be considered equivalent systems to reveal an interpretable commonality: both exhibit high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics whose behavior can only be modeled imperfectly. This creates a shared challenge whenever we seek to intervene in their dynamics. A controller may either rely on a model identified around a particular operating regime and risk failure as the system moves away from that regime, or explicitly account for uncertainty and a changing internal state. Neurostimulation has confronted an analogous problem for decades. The limitations of fixed, open-loop stimulation have motivated closed-loop and state-dependent approaches that condition intervention on the ongoing state of the neural system. Inspired by this principle, we formulate LLM steering as a feedback-control problem in which the network's internal state, rather than a fixed intervention alone, determines how the system is perturbed. We further account for the mismatch between tractable dynamical models and the nonlinear transformer they approximate through robust control. As one principled realization of this framework, we develop an $H_\infty$-based steering controller that limits sensitivity to disturbances and linearization mismatches. Across multiple steering benchmarks, the controller improves performance over existing baselines, with particularly pronounced gains under out-of-distribution conditions and varying length complexities. Beyond controller performance, we introduce robust steerability, $S_{\mathrm{rob}}$, a pre-deployment measure of tolerance to dynamics mismatch; across model-task pairs, it exhibits task-specific structure and varies systematically with model scale. Beyond robust LLM steering, our measure asks not merely whether control is possible, but how much uncertainty it can survive; a principle that may span artificial and biological neural systems.

    Synchronized Hearts, Desynchronized Brains: A Nonlinear Dynamics Perspective for Function and Failure

    arXiv:2611.06618v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Both the heart and the brain are built from electrically excitable cells, yet the emergent dynamics of these organs could not differ more. In the myocardium, a coherent traveling wave of action potentials ensures coordinated contraction; in the cerebral cortex, distributed, irregular spiking underlies efficient information processing and the generation of behavior. Here, we illuminate the principles that underlie the emergence of such distinct nonlinear dynamics using concepts from complex systems. We trace these principles across scales from ion channels and cellular action potential generation, through cellular coupling mechanisms and network architecture, to organ level dynamics and clinical phenotypes. Designed to support either synchronized waves or desynchronized information processing, the heart and the brain can thus be understood as operating on opposite sides of a synchronization phase transition. Crossing this transition implies pathological dynamics in both organs: Loss of synchrony in the heart produces arrhythmia; excess synchrony in the brain leads to epileptic seizures. We discuss how adaptation mechanisms, including plasticity, modulation, remodeling, and learning, maintain the appropriate level of (de)synchronization. Overall, we provide a unified excitable-system perspective, together with an educational notebook, to foster cross-disciplinary insights into cardiac and neural function and pathology.
